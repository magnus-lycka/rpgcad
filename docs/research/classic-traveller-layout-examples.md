# Referee-supplied classic Traveller examples

Inspected 2026-10-09. The Referee supplied two photographs as examples of classic Traveller layouts. These are visual references, not a request to reproduce either plan or to replace the current building. Publication/edition, physical scale and reuse permissions have not been established from the photographs. They are separate from the Pearce/Smith catalogue's provenance and license.

## Azhanti High Lightning — Small Craft

Source photograph: `tmp/20261009_013219.jpg` (temporary, untracked reference).

The visible legend distinguishes heavy bulkheads from thin walls/partitions, maintenance hatches, interior doors and lift shafts. Separate iris-valve and hatch columns distinguish horizontal/wall access, overhead access, floor access and combined overhead/floor access. Dashed circles denote overhead access; solid circles denote floor access. These marks do not describe open/closed state.

Color and texture carry functional distinctions: pressurized personnel space, vacuum, fuel, heavy equipment, structural members, computers/controls, machinery, weapon beams and open flooring. The photograph's lighting is not a reliable color specification.

The visible plan uses a persistent square grid, thick boundaries and simple access symbols, with equipment represented as bounded areas. The legend establishes recognition without requiring detailed illustration.

## Star Port Module Number One — Hotel Complex

Source photograph: `tmp/20261009_013545.jpg` (temporary, untracked reference; sideways in the supplied photo).

This is a monochrome plan of tourist-class rooms around a corridor, with a rounded outer boundary. Thin furniture outlines remain subordinate to heavier room boundaries and a light dashed grid. Repeated beds, chairs, tables and washrooms make the room functions readable. Furniture is placed at different angles and need not follow grid lines.

Its legend names washroom, elevator, chair, barstool, command chair, computer console, monorail track and underground passage. Washrooms and lifts are also identified with short text labels. Not every legend item appears in the photographed portion of the plan.

## Implications to evaluate

These examples support a catalogue organized around recognizable object meanings, with alternate depictions and assemblies. They show that color-coded technical plans and sparse monochrome furnished plans can both be useful references. They do not establish a single mandatory Traveller symbol standard.

Keep an object's meaning and behavior separate from its drawing convention. An overhead hatch's dashed outline is not an open door; a lift can be represented by a solid shaft mark in one convention and a labelled enclosure in another. Neither photograph establishes sight-blocking rules.

Maintain visual hierarchy between structural boundaries, access symbols, furniture and grid. Reuse identifiable furniture and fixtures while allowing rotation, off-grid placement, and larger room assemblies. These are observations and design implications, not newly agreed implementation requirements. Existing Referee-approved conventions for the current building remain in force.

## Referee preference following inspection

The Referee likes the Azhanti plan’s warm colors and wants themes/styling applicable to the same model, with black-and-white or low-color output preferable for printing. This is now recorded as a requested direction in the design notes; no exact palette or theme implementation has been chosen.
